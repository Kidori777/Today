The Lord’s Table Meeting · Chapter 5

The Advanced Meaning of the Lord’s Table Meeting

The feast of the processed Triune God

Hebrews 7:1; 6:20; Genesis 14:18; 1 Corinthians 5:7-8; Luke 22:30; 1 Corinthians 10:16, 18, 7, 14, 21; 11:26, 29

IThe Lord’s table meeting, which is the feast of the processed Triune God who supplies us, is related to the priesthood of Christ according to the order of Melchizedek.
AOur Lord Jesus has become a High Priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek—Heb. 5:6, 10; 6:20; 7:15-17; Zech. 6:13.
BMelchizedek, the king of Salem (peace) and the king of righteousness, supplied Abraham with bread and wine (Gen. 14:18), and the Lord Jesus also supplied His disciples with bread and wine.
1The ground on which we can come to the Lord’s table is righteousness and peace (Rom. 3—5).
aRighteousness has been given to us, and we have been justified.
bAs a result, we enjoy peace.
2The bread and wine on the table signify the cooked God, the embodiment of God who has been processed in order to be dispensed into us.
aThe bread signifies the life supply, and the wine signifies the redeeming blood that solves our thirst.
bTo solve our thirst, which comes from being under God’s condemnation, Christ passed through the process of death on the cross to be the redeeming wine (the redeeming juice of life—Lev. 17:11) as our life supply.
3Melchizedek’s coming to supply Abraham, the forefather of the called race, with bread and wine signifies Christ’s coming to supply Himself into us as the processed God.
CToday our Christ is not a High Priest who offers sacrifices but a High Priest who supplies bread and wine.
1Christ’s eternal priesthood and His ministry in the heavens are for God’s original purpose of dispensing and distributing God Himself into us for His corporate expression.
2He has redeemed us, and now He feeds us.
3We need to come to the throne of grace, to our Melchizedek, who dispenses the processed God as grace into us to be our daily supply for our enjoyment.
DChrist, our great High Priest typified by Melchizedek, intercedes for us and dispenses the processed God, with divine blessings, to those who fight for God’s interests as Abraham did—Gen. 14:17-24; Heb. 7:1, 25.
IIThe Lord established His table with the bread and the cup to replace the feast of the Passover, in order to fulfill the type and to be the real Passover to us—Matt. 26:17-30; 1 Cor. 5:7.
AThe Passover table was the table of the Old Testament economy (Matt. 26:17-25), but the Lord’s table, the fulfillment and replacement of the Passover, is the table of the New Testament economy (vv. 26-30).
BNow we are keeping the real feast of unleavened bread—Matt. 26:17; 1 Cor. 5:8.
1The Feast of Unleavened Bread was a seven-day feast (Lev. 23:5) and was called the Passover—Luke 22:1; Mark 14:1.
2In fact, the feast of the Passover was the first day of the Feast of Unleavened Bread—Exo. 12:15-20.
COther meanings of the Lord’s table typified by the Passover
1Since the Passover was in the first month of the year, it signifies a new beginning (Exo. 12:2; 13:4). At the Lord’s table we need to be reminded that we need a new beginning, the new beginning of the eighth day, which is the first day of the week and the day of resurrection.
2At the Lord’s table, as typified by the Passover, we should remember that we were those who should have died under God’s righteous judgment, but God prepared Christ to be the substitute who could die for us.
3At the Lord’s table, as typified by the Passover, we should be reminded that we do not belong to ourselves but to the One who died for us, and that we should now enjoy Him as our eternal life.
4At the Lord’s table, as typified by the Passover, we participate in the feast to enjoy Christ in God’s presence, and this enjoyment becomes the mutual satisfaction of God and us.
DThe feast of the Passover, the feast of the Lord’s table, and the feast of the kingdom (Luke 22:16; Matt. 26:29) are actually one feast in three stages: the Old Testament stage, the New Testament stage, and the kingdom stage.
1The Lord’s table is the replacement and continuation of the Old Testament feast of the Passover.
2The feast of the Lord’s table in the New Testament will be replaced and continued by the feast in the coming kingdom of God, when the Savior feasts with the overcoming saints—Luke 22:30; 13:28-29.
IIIWhenever we come to the Lord’s table to enjoy Christ as the all-inclusive One, we are in the good land, enjoying all its riches in our experience—Deut. 8:7-11; 1 Cor. 1:9; 10:16.
AChrist as our table is typified by the good land, which became a table to the children of Israel. When they dwelt in the good land, they feasted on the table, enjoying all the rich produce of the land.
BEnjoying the riches of Christ as our table issues in living Christ, defeating the enemy, establishing the kingdom of God, and building the house of God in order to fulfill His eternal purpose.
IVWhat we enjoy at the Lord’s table is typified by Israel’s eating of the offerings at the altar—1 Cor. 10:18.
AThe fellowship of the altar is a type of the fellowship of the blood and the body of Christ.
BThose who had fellowship at the altar by eating the sacrifices are a type of those who have fellowship at the Lord’s table by eating Jesus (John 6:57), the reality of the sacrifices.
CThose who have fellowship are also co-partakers.
1To partake of the table is to enjoy the Lord and to be one with the Lord.
2Eating makes the one who eats one with what he eats.
VThe Lord’s table is a symbol of the New Testament jubilee, in which we enjoy God’s salvation release from all the bondage of sin, together with the Triune God who is our life and blessing—Luke 4:18-19; Lev. 25:8-17.
VITo partake of the Lord’s table is to get out of idolatry—1 Cor. 10:14.
AIn 1 Corinthians 10:14-22 Paul speaks concerning the Lord’s table, but he begins this section with a command to flee from idolatry.
BIdolatry is the enjoyment of something in place of the Lord (1 Cor. 10:7), but the Lord’s table is the genuine enjoyment of the Lord Himself.
CUltimately, in the whole universe there are only two tables: the table of demons, which makes people one with demons, and the Lord’s table, which makes believers one with the Lord.
DThe Lord’s table is not merely a matter of gathering together on the Lord’s Day; this table should be our enjoyment daily and even continually.
1Do not eat anything other than the Lord, and do not enjoy anything that replaces Him.
2We should not enjoy anything other than Christ, who is our table, our feast, and our land.
VIIThe Lord’s supper should remind us that we live on this earth for the Lord’s satisfaction.
AAt the Lord’s table we take His body and blood as our satisfaction, but at the Lord’s supper we offer our remembrance to Him for His enjoyment.
BBy eating the bread and drinking the cup we “proclaim the Lord’s death until He comes”—1 Cor. 11:26.
1The church bridges the gap between the Lord’s first coming to accomplish an all-inclusive redemption through death and His second coming to establish the kingdom of God.
2Christ’s death produced the church, and the church will bring in the kingdom for the Lord’s satisfaction.
3Eating the supper reminds us that in the church we should cooperate with Christ, who is working in the heavens to bring in the kingdom for His satisfaction, and live a life in oneness with Him.
VIIIAt the Lord’s table we offer Christ to God the Father as the peace offering—Lev. 3:1-5, 6-7, 11-12, 16; 7:11-13, 20-21.
AWhen our trespasses and sins have been dealt with by Christ as the trespass offering and the sin offering, and when God and we are satisfied with Christ as the burnt offering and the meal offering, we can offer Christ as the peace offering to God the Father for mutual enjoyment in peace.
1In order to enjoy Christ as the peace offering at the Lord’s table, we need the covering of the Lord’s redeeming and cleansing blood—Lev. 3:2; 1 Pet. 1:2; Heb. 12:24.
2If we are unclean, we cannot partake of the peace offering. All uncleanness must be dealt with—Lev. 7:20-21; 1 Cor. 6:11.
BThe Christ whom we offer to the Father as the peace offering is for the enjoyment of all the parties who participate in the economic worship with God.
XWhen we eat the Lord’s supper, we satisfy Him by remembering Him, and we care for God’s administration by discerning the Body—1 Cor. 11:29.
AThe unique and mysterious Body of Christ is the means by which God carries out His administration.
BWhen we go to the Lord’s supper, we need to discern the Body in order to judge whether the bread on the table represents the unique and mysterious Body of Christ or some division of men (some denomination).
COur partaking of the Lord’s table should be the unique fellowship of His unique Body, without any division either in practice or in spirit.
DIn order for God’s administration to be carried out, we need to care for the oneness of the unique and mysterious Body of Christ.
